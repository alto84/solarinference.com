The node

A quiet box in the yard.

One datacenter-class server in a sealed, cooled enclosure about the size of an air-conditioning unit. We own it, install it, insure it and run it remotely. AI teams rent its computing by the hour.

It sits behind its own meter and its own internet line, and never touches the home’s network.

Permission

Town by town, with the utility.

Most town codes never imagined a computer in the back yard. We take that on ourselves, one town at a time.

  1. Read the code

    Home-business rules, accessory equipment, and any data-center definition or ban.

  2. Ask for a ruling

    That the unit is accessory equipment, like a generator or an AC condenser.

  3. Meet the standards

    Setbacks, screening, permits, and the night noise limit at the property line.

  4. Write the rule

    Work with the town on a simple standard for home compute, as towns did for solar.

Questions

What a homeowner asks first.

Will the neighbors hear it?

It’s designed to stay under New Jersey’s night limit at the property line, 50 decibels, about the sound of a quiet office.

What if the power goes out?

The battery backs up the house. The computer shuts itself down first, so the house gets the power.

Is it safe?

Listed equipment on its own circuits, watched around the clock for heat and smoke, with remote shutoff. We insure it, and our liability policy covers the home.

Can it see our network?

No. It has its own fiber line and never joins the home network. We never look at what customers run on it.

Do we need a bigger electrical service?

Usually not. Smart power throttling watches the home’s total draw and turns the node down first, so the house always gets what it needs and the service stays within its rating. The battery covers short peaks.

Does it raise our electric bill?

No. The node’s power is on its own meter or included in our fee, never on the household’s bill.

Do we have to do anything?

No. We monitor and repair it remotely, with a few scheduled visits a year. You only keep the circuit on and let us in on notice.

What if we sell the house?

The agreement ends, and we remove the box within days.

Is our town allowed?

That’s the first thing we check. If your town’s code is silent, we ask for a ruling. If it has banned data centers, we won’t place a node there.
